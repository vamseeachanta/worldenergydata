Living cost-basis time-series for field-development economics

The FDAS cost deck rests on single-point assumptions. This dataset replaces them with a sourced, time-varying, inflation-normalized basis — a bottom-up component series (day rates, indices) cross-checked against a top-down anchor of disclosed sanctioned-project totals. Every figure on this page is colour-coded by how we know it. Nothing here is a guess: where we lack a source, the cell is blank and listed as a gap, never filled with a plausible number.

3. Inflation normalization — nominal vs real (scope addition #1)

CPI deflator: U.S. CPI-U all items (FRED CPIAUCSL), calendar-year mean of monthly observations. Complete measured series; no interpolation.

UCCI deflator: IHS/S&P Upstream Capital Costs Index (2000=100). Proprietary, with no public endpoint — built from 9 publicly sourced anchor year(s) spanning 2006–2020, with linear interpolation across 1 gap year(s). Quarterly prints within a year are averaged. NOT extrapolated beyond the anchor range. NOT BRIDGED — gaps wider than 3 years are left uncovered rather than interpolated, because a straight line across them would erase real structural breaks: 2014–2018 (between anchors 2013 and 2019). UCCI-real is not published for those years.

Real (deflated) cost

realb = nominalt × ( Db / Dt )
D = the deflator index. Two bases are published and never averaged: CPI (general purchasing power) and UCCI (upstream sector). Basis year b = 2025. Deflating by CPI and by UCCI answer different questions and routinely disagree in sign — that disagreement is a finding, not a bug.

Did it beat inflation?

excess = CAGR(component) − CAGR(deflator)
Both CAGRs are taken over the component's own first→last sourced year, so the verdict is anchored on real data at both ends rather than on a fitted value. Positive excess = the component outpaced the yardstick.

5. Back-allocation to lifecycle stages + bottom-up reconciliation

Stage back-allocation

costs = CAPEXtotal × ŝs ,   ŝs = ss·τs / Σk sk·τk
s = the prior share for the development type; τ = the well-count tilt, applied to drill+complete only: τ = 1 + 0.5·(wells/reference − 1), clamped to [0.5, 1.8]. The 0.5 makes the tilt sub-linear — incremental wells on one campaign are cheaper than the first, because rig mob is already paid. Shares renormalise to 1, so the disclosed total is always conserved exactly.

Uncertainty band

costslo,hi = CAPEXtotal × (ŝs ∓ bs)
b = the prior's band, typically ±8–10 share points. Operators disclose a total and a scope, almost never a stage breakdown — so the shares are priors, not measurements. A single-point stage cost here would be a lie of precision; the band is the honest output. Where an operator does disclose a split, that overrides the prior and the band collapses to zero.

Bottom-up reconciliation

drillbu = Nwells × days/well × raterig × m
m = the spread multiplier grossing the bare rig rate up to a total well cost (tubulars, mud, cement, logging, ROV/vessel support, operator overhead). m = 2.0 is the conventional planning number and is an assumption. The gap between this and the allocated drill slice is the deliverable — we report it, we do not tune it away.

6. Reconciliation — do the two series agree?

Implied days per well (the inversion)

daysimplied = drillallocated / ( Nwells × raterig × m )
m = 2.0, the spread multiplier grossing a bare rig rate up to a total well cost (tubulars, mud, cement, logging, ROV/vessel support, operator overhead). m is an assumption, and every number in this table scales inversely with it. The rig rate is the sourced drillship market average for the FID year (nearest sourced year within 3).

Result: the 18 reconcilable projects imply a median of 132 days per well, and 14 of 18 fall in the 90–280 day band that a deepwater well plausibly takes. The top-down totals and the bottom-up day-rate series are therefore broadly consistent — the disclosed CAPEX of these projects is compatible with the rig rates we sourced, at a believable drilling duration. That is a genuine, if modest, corroboration of both series.

An emergent finding worth the whole exercise. The outliers below are not noise — they are regional. Back-allocating disclosed CAPEX alone puts Guyana at $70MM per well (n=7) against $164MM per well in the US GOM (n=7) — a 2.4x spread. That is a real and well-documented industry fact (Guyana's Stabroek wells are drilled fast in benign, high-quality reservoir on a batch programme; GOM Paleogene wells are 20k-psi, slow and brutal), and we did not put it in — it fell out of the disclosed totals. The bottom-up and top-down series independently recovering a known truth is the strongest corroboration this dataset currently has.

Rows outside the plausible band are coloured. Note this is a weak check: it uses assumed priors on one side and an assumed spread multiplier on the other, so it can only catch order-of-magnitude errors — not 20% ones. Its value is that it would have loudly caught a 10x mistake, and that the regional structure it surfaces is checkable against reality. A single global days/well number would be meaningless — that is itself a finding: the deck must carry a per-region drilling duration, not one constant.

7. Fitted trend curves per component (scope addition #3)

Four candidate forms are fitted per component and the winner is chosen by adjusted R², so a richer model has to earn its extra parameter. When oil_linked wins, that is the finding: the component is priced off the cycle, not off the calendar, and extrapolating it in time alone would be meaningless.

A fitted value is not a datum. Every value this produces is stamped fitted and years outside the fitted window are additionally flagged as extrapolated.

8. Not yet covered

Stated plainly, because a living dataset that hides its own holes is worse than no dataset. Milestone 1 does not yet cover:

  • UCCI/UOCI full history — proprietary to S&P Global. Only scattered published values are sourceable; the sector-deflator basis is therefore anchor-and-interpolate and is not extrapolated beyond its anchors.
  • SURF $/km and host CAPEX $/tonne component series — these are contracted lump-sum, so public day-rate equivalents largely do not exist. The back-allocation in section 5 is currently the only route to them.
  • Heavy-lift and pipelay day rates — same reason: contracted lump-sum, not chartered at a published rate.
  • OPEX ($/bbl variable, $/yr fixed) — not yet sourced as a time-series.
  • Fiscal terms (royalty bands, tax) as a time-varying series.
  • Integration into the FDAS model as a time-varying deck (issue #651) — this milestone builds the basis; wiring it into the model is the next step.
