How to read this

Every figure in the underlying dataset carries a provenance tag. This page uses only the first kind above the register:

sourced  read off a citable page — the quote, URL and access date are in the dataset ·  assumed  engineering judgement — appears ONLY in section 5, never in a chart here ·  todo  a known gap — rendered blank, never zero.

Dots on the charts are actual observations; a line through three dots is three facts, not a dense series. Hover any dot for its value. Full citations: curated CSVs.

4 · What the data alone says

Day rates move with the oil price, not the calendar

Same-year correlation between each sourced day-rate series and sourced Brent annual means — plain Pearson r, no model:

ComponentPearson r vs Brent (same-year)Overlapping years
Drillship day rate+0.3013
Semi-sub day rate+0.3811
Jackup day rate+0.897
OSV/PSV day rate+0.6326
AHTS day rate+0.6216

Same-year r understates the link where rates reprice with a delay — deepwater rigs sit on multi-year contracts, so the drillship figure is low by construction; shorter-cycle jackups and OSVs track the price almost directly. The lagged analysis lives in the technical report and is part of decision A3. Consequence either way: filling gap years by extrapolating in time alone would be meaningless.

The disclosed totals imply very different well costs by region

Straight CAPEX-per-well from section 3 already separates regions before any allocation: batch-drilled Guyana projects sit far below US Gulf of Mexico 20k-psi Paleogene developments. This spread is documented in the trade press and falls out of the operators' own totals here — two independent routes to the same fact. Consequence for the model: per-region drilling durations (decision A4), not one global constant.

Support vessels outran CPI; rigs mostly tracked it

From the verdicts table: OSV/PSV and AHTS day rates grew ~4–5 pp/yr faster than CPI over their sourced windows, while semi-sub rates tracked CPI and drillships sit in between. Cost escalation is not uniform across components — a single inflation factor would misprice the mix.

5 · Assumptions register — nothing above uses these

Four judgement calls stand between the sourced record and a usable cost deck. Each is a named decision awaiting sign-off; none is silently embedded in the charts or tables above.

A1

Stage-share priors

How a sanctioned total splits across drill / complete / SURF / host / install / hookup. Engineering judgement, banded ±8–10 points, flagged by the authoring run itself as the dataset's weak point.

What it would touch: Splitting project totals into per-stage costs (the stage_allocations table and everything downstream of it).

⏳ awaiting approval — decision recorded on issue #1017

A2

Deflator policy

Which yardstick is the default 'real' basis — general CPI or the sector index UCCI. This page shows CPI; UCCI is computed and available.

What it would touch: Every real-terms number and every OUTPACED/LAGGED verdict.

⏳ awaiting approval — decision recorded on issue #1017

A3

Gap-fill method

Years with no source are filled by oil-price-linked fitted curves — never by drawing a line across a blackout (the UCCI 2014–18 gap stays open until this is approved).

What it would touch: Any figure for a year without a direct source.

⏳ awaiting approval — decision recorded on issue #1017

A4

Per-region drilling durations

The disclosed totals imply very different well costs by region (section 3). Turning that into the model means region-specific days-per-well instead of one global constant.

What it would touch: The FDAS time-varying deck (#651) — the first consumer of this dataset.

⏳ awaiting approval — decision recorded on issue #1017
