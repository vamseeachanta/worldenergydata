Gulf of Mexico well permits: a public US data file

New-well permits in US federal waters of the Gulf of Mexico are far fewer each year than in the mid-2000s, and the permits have moved from jack-ups in shallow water to drillships in deep water. The public files of the US Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement let your team check this and add its own questions, such as permit-day estimates by water depth.

1 Public permit data

Public data shows how new-well permits in US federal waters of the Gulf of America (formerly the Gulf of Mexico) have changed since 2005. The counts come from the Application for Permit to Drill (APD) raw data of the US Bureau of Safety and Environmental Enforcement (BSEE). The map and section 3 use its borehole and Application for Permit to Modify (APM) raw data.

Some of the observations are as follows:

  • @@MAP_OBSERVATION@@ (Figure 1).
  • Each year now has far fewer permits than in the mid-2000s (Figure 2).
  • Most permits once named a jack-up rig; since 2015, most name a drillship (Figure 2).
  • Permits in less than 100 m fell the most. Those in 500 m or more changed far less; since 2015, they are most permits (Figure 4).

Figure 1 shows where new wells started, coloured by water depth, in 2005 to 2009 and in 2021 to 2025. The buttons above the map switch between the two periods; the legend turns each water-depth band on or off.

The raw data page of the BSEE Data Center lists the files used here, and files on platform structures, pipelines, production and offshore statistics by water depth.

2 Well permits by rig type and water depth

The permit file holds each application for a permit to drill in US federal waters. Each record gives:

  • The permit type.
  • The requested spud date: the date drilling is to start.
  • The water depth.
  • The rig name and rig type.

The counting rules are:

  • Count each well once, at its first New Well permit in the Gulf of Mexico region.
  • Use the requested spud year and the rig type named on that permit.

From 2005 to 2026, 2,895 wells are counted this way. The rule leaves out each well's later permits, and the 10 wells whose first New Well permit gives no requested spud date. The year 2026 runs only to the file of 2 November 2026.

2.1 Rig type

The counting script names six rig types:

  • Jack-up.
  • Drillship.
  • Semisubmersible.
  • DP semisubmersible: dynamically positioned, held on station by its thrusters.
  • Platform rig.
  • Submersible.

Barge, barge rig and well intervention vessel (19 permits) are grouped as other. Figure 2 shows the new-well permits each year by rig type. Figure 3 shows the shares in 2005, 2015 and 2025, one full year per decade.

2.2 Water depth

Every permit counted records a water depth. The file gives it in feet; this page gives it in metres (1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly, NIST SP 811, Appendix B.8). Figure 4 shows the new-well permits each year in four water-depth bands: under 100 m, 100 to 500 m, 500 to 1,500 m, and 1,500 m and deeper. A depth on a band edge is counted in the deeper band. Figure 5 shows the shares in the same three years.

3.2 Counting rules and exclusions

  • Drilling days are the recorded dates from spud to total depth of each original borehole (borehole file).
  • Days on completion, intervention and abandonment permits are the operator's estimate on each permit (APM file).
  • Each original borehole or permit is counted once, for 2005 to 2025, in the same four water-depth bands.
  • Missing, invalid, zero or negative days are left out and counted.
  • The APD file holds no estimated drilling days.

4 Outlook and open points

4.1 Our hypotheses

Our hypothesis: with few new-well permits now naming a jack-up (Figure 2), jack-up work in US federal Gulf of Mexico waters will turn further from new wells to plugging and abandonment.

Our hypothesis: with deep-water permits falling far less than shallow-water permits (Figure 4), new-well engineering in these waters will centre further on deep-water drilling, risers and subsea equipment.

4.2 Open points

  • Each count takes the rig type named in a well's first permit. How often a different type of rig drilled the well is open.
  • Figure 6 shows recorded drilling intervals and operator estimates side by side; they come from different records and are not paired.
  • The medians describe 2005 to 2025 as a whole.

Appendix B Counting rules and record counts for section 3

B.1 Recorded intervals and estimates

  • Borehole: TOTAL_DEPTH_DATE minus WELL_SPUD_DATE gives a recorded spud-to-total-depth calendar interval.
  • APM: EST_OPERATION_DAYS is the operator's estimate on the permit.
  • APD: in the permit file retrieved @@SCHEMA_DATE@@, neither inspected APDRawData table has an estimated-days field. No drilling estimate is calculated from that file.

Figure 6 and Table B1 use full cohort years 2005 to 2025.

B.2 Detailed counting rules and exclusions

  • Borehole: count one API_WELL_NUMBER with REGION_CODE=G, original-hole suffix ST00BP00 and status other than CNL. Select by spud year, 2005 to 2025. Use borehole WATER_DEPTH. The map uses the same boreholes, spudded in 2005 to 2009 or 2021 to 2025, at SURF_LATITUDE and SURF_LONGITUDE; the map selection counts give the wells without coordinates in the map area.
  • Subtract dates without adding a day. A recorded total-depth date after 2025 remains in the spud-year cohort. Missing total-depth dates exclude unfinished or unreported intervals; results describe recorded completed intervals. Zero-day intervals may represent work within one day; they are excluded under the nonpositive-day rule. The audit separates zero, negative, blank-date and invalid-date cases.
  • APM: count each SN_APM once. Duplicate rows must agree on API, status date, operation code, days and depth. Conflicts stop the build. Separate submissions for the same well remain separate observations; repeat API, operation and year keys are counted as a diagnostic below.
  • Match the APM API_WELL_NUMBER exactly to an unambiguous borehole REGION_CODE=G. Exclude unmatched or conflicting regions. Use APM WATER_DEPTH.
  • Select APM ACC_STATUS_DATE years 2005 to 2025. The file has no separate approval-status field; approved-only coverage is not established.
  • Completion: COMP without ZONE. Intervention: WORKOVER, ENHANCE, or COMP with ZONE. This groups workover, production enhancement and recompletion. Completion does not mean initial completion only. ZONE takes precedence even when INITCOMP also occurs.
  • Abandonment: ABANDON with PERMABAN or TEMPABAN. Permanent takes precedence if both occur. The estimate covers the whole submission, not an isolated subtype.
  • Exclude any PLUGBACK subtype as new-hole work. Exclude unspecified abandonment and OTHER, INFORM and UTIL; their physical scope is unresolved. Unknown primary codes stop the build.
  • The code mapping retains the source descriptions and analysis rules. Subtypes join through SN_APM_FK; they do not multiply observations. Classification uses codes, not free-text narratives. The subtype cross-tab shows the assigned activity. A submission can have several subtypes, so its rows are not additive.
  • Convert feet to metres at 1 ft = 0.3048 m exactly (NIST SP 811, Appendix B.8). Use under 100 m, 100 to 500 m, 500 to 1,500 m, and 1,500 m and deeper. Band edges go to the deeper band.
  • Exclude missing, invalid, infinite and nonpositive days from each median. Count those exclusions in Table B1 and days-summary.csv. Missing or invalid depth remains a separate row outside the four depth bands.
  • Use linear interpolation for the 25th and 75th percentiles. Pool the full period without trimming large positive intervals. A missing median means no usable observations, not zero days.

No cost data is used.

The repeat-key sensitivity check also keeps only the latest status date per API, primary operation and status year. A date tie uses the last SN_APM in text order.
