1. Executive Summary

The federal acreage module is an offline screening tool for lease-record coverage on a regular survey-cell grid. Analysis is performed to determine the supported calculation and evidence gaps; key results are summarized below.

  • The documented synthetic tract has 320 acres gross and 280 acres of supplied-record coverage.
    • The 40 acres without supplied records identify an evidence gap. They do not establish land availability or title.
  • The example returns 200 acres in the active-lease union and 80 acres in the expired-or-nonleased union.
    • The result is partial evidence because exceptions and uncovered tract area remain. These quantities support checking the supplied records against the stated idealized geometry.
  • Real-tract decisions require additional evidence.
    • Survey geometry, current source records, the acreage-unit convention and county title records require assessment before the screening quantities are used for acquisition or ownership decisions.

The example is developed in Section 6 from the public repository example. Its records are authored synthetic data, not a public land-record extract.

2. Introduction and scope

Tract cells are intersected with supplied federal lease or parcel records, and coverage is aggregated without counting overlapping cells twice. Record exceptions and source references are carried beside the acreage quantities.

The main workflow comprises a fixture provider, validated input models, the public evaluation function and paired structured-data and HTML outputs. A source-provider protocol defines the interface for future acquisition. The available router accepts fixture acquisition only (public interfaces; provider and router).

2.1 Not covered

Live acquisition, irregular or fractional survey lots, multiple sections, polygon clipping, coordinate transformations, net revenue interests, mineral ownership and title-chain assessment are not provided by this workflow. No physical plant, loading model or licensed numerical solver is used. The module name does not establish these capabilities.

3. Basis and criteria

The calculation basis is the documented synthetic example and the module's equal-area survey-cell convention. Numerical consistency is assessed separately from the accuracy of a land survey or lease register.

3.1 Geometry and units

Each accepted quarter-quarter cell is assigned exactly 40 acres by the model. The federal surveying manual describes nominal subdivision areas, with a section of 640 acres, a quarter section of 160 acres and a quarter-quarter of 40 acres (2009 edition, Chapter III, paragraph 3-32, p. 47). This nominal basis does not establish the measured acreage of an irregular real section.

The hectare record is interpreted using the international-foot acre for this arithmetic demonstration (unit-conversion table). Its applicability to any historical source record is NOT_EVALUATED. Source-specific unit evidence is required before that conversion is applied to real records.

3.2 Software criteria

Converted record area is compared with the area of its listed cells. A difference greater than 0.5 acre produces an ambiguous-unit exception; this is the current software policy, not a cadastral accuracy standard (area inspection function). The inclusive boundary is not rejected by this test.

The chart's upper comparator is the gross tract area. No engineering operating envelope or statutory land-availability threshold is inferred. The area bound follows because every intersection is a subset of the tract (set operations).

Hand-worked acreage anchors and displayed-input arithmetic are compared with an absolute tolerance of 10−9 acre in the report checks. This numerical checking tolerance is not a measurement uncertainty (report checks).

4. Inputs and outputs

Inputs are validated before evaluation. Acreage values are returned together with their availability state and source reference, so an unknown quantity remains distinguishable from a true zero.

4.1 Input contract

The tract supplies its identifier, descriptive label, state, principal meridian, township, range, section, distinct quarter-quarter cells and gross area. Gross area equals the cell count multiplied by the fixed cell area. Each record supplies a distinct serial, record type and status, area and units, geometry or an explicit missing-geometry value, source identifier and optional effective and expiry dates.

Every source supplies a unique identifier, dataset description, fixture-or-live classification, timezone-aware retrieval time and licence note. Duplicate serials, missing source references, unsupported aliquot labels, non-finite areas and reversed date intervals are rejected by the public contracts (models; evaluation).

4.2 Output contract

The result contains gross area, record coverage, active-lease area, expired-or-nonleased area and derived no-record area, together with per-record converted and intersecting areas, exceptions, sources, an evidence disposition and a separate title-chain gap. The serializer writes a structured-data file and an escaped HTML report from that result (output writer).

Complete evidence means full supplied-record coverage without exceptions. Partial evidence means coverage exists but exceptions or uncovered area remain. Blocked evidence means no intersecting geometry is available; every rollup quantity, including gross area, is then unavailable in the returned rollup. None of these dispositions establishes title.

5. Theory and method

Acreage is calculated by counting distinct intersecting cells. The equations below express finite-set operations on the idealized equal-area grid, rather than a survey adjustment.

5.1 Geometry and symbols

Let T be the tract's set of cells, Ri the cells listed by record i, a the area assigned to each cell, and |S| the number of distinct cells in set S. The intersection symbol ∩ means membership in both sets; ∪ means membership in either set; the empty set ∅ has no cells. North, south, east and west are abbreviated N, S, E and W; a label such as NE-NW means the northeast quarter of the northwest quarter.

The nominal equal-cell relation is a = 640 acres / (4 × 4) = 40 acres (2009 surveying manual, Chapter III, paragraph 3-32, p. 47). The model enforces that value exactly; physical sections can depart from the nominal subdivision.

5.2 Coverage and lease categories

For each record, Ii = T ∩ Ri. The coverage set is C = ∪ Ii, and coverage area is AC = a |C|. Gross tract area is AT = a |T|, and no-record area is AU = AT − AC. These identities follow from distinct-cell membership and cardinality (Contemporary Mathematics, 2023 edition, Section 1.4); their application assumes equal cell areas.

The active-lease set L is the union of intersections for records with active lease status and resolved date and unit checks. The expired-or-nonleased set E is the corresponding union for other resolved records, including active parcel records. Reported category areas are a |L| and a |E|. If L ∩ E ≠ ∅, or intersecting records have unresolved dates or units, both category areas become ambiguous; the geometric coverage remains available (category policy).

A pairwise overlap has area a |Ii ∩ Ij|, using the same cited intersection definition. An overlap exception does not add area to C. Missing geometry is excluded; geometry outside the tract gives no intersection. The expired-or-nonleased category is not a finding that the land is available for leasing.

5.3 Unit conversion and time basis

For hectare input H, the conversion basis is A = H × 10,000 / [43,560 × (0.3048)²] acres. The hectare supplies 10,000 square metres (SI Brochure, ninth edition, version 4.01, Section 4, Table 8); the acre contains 43,560 square feet and the international foot is 0.3048 metre (2026 revision, acre and foot rows). The module uses a rounded coefficient for this ratio; the report calls that implementation.

Date checks use each record source's retrieval date. An active record is unresolved when it expires before that date or becomes effective after it; pending and unknown status are unresolved. The aggregate data date is the earliest supplied retrieval timestamp, not the current date. Retrieval time is not proof of when a legal change took effect (date and provenance policy).

6. Worked example

The documented public repository example is evaluated through the public provider and evaluation function. Its authored synthetic records demonstrate the calculation; no live record extract is represented.

The fixture's data date is 2026-07-10. Its 8 tract cells give 320 acres, and 6 supplied records are assessed. The returned union covers 280 acres, with 200 acres in active leases and 80 acres in expired-or-nonleased records. The derived no-record area is 320 − 280 = 40 acres (documented example).

The hectare input is 16.1874 hectares. Its returned converted area is 39.9999 acres at the displayed precision; the geometric intersection remains 40 acres. The input keeps its original precision and the difference remains below the 0.5-acre software criterion (conversion basis; area policy).

The result is partial evidence. There are 3 exceptions: an out-of-tract record, missing geometry and a repeated active cell with 40 acres of overlap. The repeated area is counted once in coverage. The 40 acres without supplied records remain an evidence gap. Title-chain assessment is NOT_EVALUATED.

Record-level inputs and intersections are tabulated in the appendix. The existing example's saved outputs and the fresh evaluation are compared by the module tests.

7. Validation evidence

The repository holds synthetic fixtures, a published worked description and tests of the existing contracts, calculation and serializer. These records establish bounded software checks; survey accuracy and source-register completeness are NOT_EVALUATED.

7.1 Existing evidence

Calculation tests cover coverage unions, overlap exceptions, partial intersections, absent geometry, hectare conversion, unit ambiguity, pending and unknown status, dates, empty records and complete supplied-record coverage. Model tests cover gross-area consistency, unavailable markers, structured-data round trips and reversed dates. Provider and serializer tests cover fixture licensing, router selection, deterministic paired outputs and agreement with the saved example (existing module tests).

The provider-substitution test relabels fixture sources through a compatible interface. It is not a live acquisition or federal-service integration test. The inspected module evidence contains no measured-survey benchmark, live register reconciliation or county title-chain comparison.

7.2 Report checks

Three hand-worked anchors check gross, covered and active-lease areas. Additional consistency checks bind every displayed example quantity to module results, recompute the headline from displayed inputs, compare a fresh build at displayed precision, preserve the original input bytes and bind source digests to the calculation files (report checks).

The complete HTML, change-panel data and comment functions are tested offline, including hover, keyboard focus, comment export and import. The adjacent-file save handler is exercised with a simulated folder-permission interface; a real folder-picker interaction is not established by that check. The input basis and calculation-source digests are embedded for readback. Those checks do not establish the accuracy of a real lease register.

8. Limits and recommendations

The reported quantities apply to the supplied synthetic records and the fixed equal-cell geometry. The following evidence is required to extend the use to real tracts.

8.1 Limits of use

Acreage is nominal, not a surveyed polygon area. Irregular lots, cross-section geometry, boundary disputes and source acreage-unit differences are unresolved. Missing records do not establish an unleased or nonfederal tract, and record coverage does not establish title. Source dates do not establish continuing legal validity.

Known geometry still contributes to coverage when a record's date or units make the lease split ambiguous. Active parcel records enter the expired-or-nonleased category. Caller-created records also carry no independent section identifier; the fixture provider checks the tract grid, while other providers require their own grid qualification (provider contract; evaluation policy).

8.2 Recommendations

Before real-tract screening, reconcile the legal description and measured survey geometry with the applicable filed plat and notes. The federal surveying manual identifies those records as the basis for locating the land (Chapter I, paragraph 1-1). Keep irregular geometry outside this model until an area-preserving geometry extension and comparison cases are supplied.

Resolve the missing geometry and out-of-tract record against their source before interpreting the uncovered area. Review the repeated cell against the source instruments; retain the union calculation while the overlap exception remains. Reconcile source units with the applicable international or historical survey-foot acre before using hectare conversions (unit-convention guidance).

Obtain a dated source snapshot and source-rights record before live acquisition is introduced. Add status-date boundary and cross-category overlap examples before changing the lease-category policy. Obtain county recorder and title-chain records for ownership decisions; the existing result explicitly reports that evidence as NOT_EVALUATED (title-gap contract).

10.2 Numerical and presentation pre-flight

The hand arithmetic is eight cells × forty acres for gross area; seven distinct covered cells × forty acres for coverage; and five distinct active-lease cells × forty acres for active leases. The repeated cell is counted once. These literals are asserted separately from the report's module-consistency checks. The headline difference is recomputed from the displayed gross and coverage values.

The report rebuild compares displayed values and rounded chart arrays. Calculation-source and input digests identify UTF-8 text with line endings normalized to line feed; the original files are not rewritten. Report text, stylesheet and revision data also bind the comment file to this document. Unicode text is read and written explicitly as UTF-8 with line-feed endings. The report tests are executed with the Python UTF-8 environment override unset. Offline browser checks exercise the chart and comment persistence. Check results and scope are recorded with the report delivery.
