# Cost method

Article: "Public Data for Gulf of America: well interventions", The Deckhand Chronicles, revision 6 of 19 Nov 2026. This page holds in full what Appendix C of the article summarises. The sources are listed in [README.md](README.md).

The spread calculation multiplies the published day rate by the scenario duration. TAP-738 Table 8-2 (p. 8-12) gives a rigless platform work charge of USD 225,856 over 84 hours, equivalent to USD 64,530 a day. Casing jacks cost USD 68,424 a day (Table 8-3, p. 8-14), and a drillship or semisubmersible costs USD 674,600 a day (p. 8-18). Both platform scenarios include the mobilisation and demobilisation charges from Table 8-2. The drillship and semisubmersible scenarios exclude rig mobilisation, for which TAP-738 provides no charge in that spread.

The low and high well-work scenarios use 7 and 14 days. The base duration of 10.5 days is their arithmetic midpoint. BOEM 2016-018 (p. 3-37) reports a Gulf median of 7 days and a range from 1 day to several months. The BOEM Alaska update (2012, p. 108) gives a range of one to two weeks.

In unescalated 2015 dollars, the shelf-platform rigless well-work scenario costs USD 0.53 to 0.98 million and the subsea drillship or semisubmersible scenario costs USD 4.7 to 9.4 million. The deepwater dry-tree abandonment row uses the published per-well total in TAP-738 Table 8-4 (p. 8-15). These are scenario estimates. The [study cost basis](https://api.github.com/repositories/574746314/contents/examples/bsee/interventions_by_depth/README.md?ref=24744b528298f26c51bfce4cb3b66fa4f676eb02) identifies the source pages and allowances, and the [published cost table](https://api.github.com/repositories/574746314/contents/examples/bsee/interventions_by_depth/output/cost_table.csv?ref=78f1a9183a75e56adb7f31e88b271864f0b69d37) gives the full-precision estimates.
