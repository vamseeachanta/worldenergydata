# Detail behind the life and water-depth sections

Article: "Public Data for Gulf of America: well interventions", The Deckhand Chronicles, revision 6 of 19 Nov 2026. This page holds in full what Appendix D of the article summarises.

- **Heavy work.** 59% of development wells drilled since 2005 have had at least one heavy intervention approved; development wells still standing from that group are a median 14.4 years old. Two in three exploration wells drilled since 2005 are already permanently abandoned, most within a year of spud.
- **Interventions by depth.** In 2025, the 765 intervention approvals were under 100 m: 316; 100 to 500 m: 99; 500 to 1,500 m: 185; 1,500 m and deeper: 165.
- **Count against rate.** The count of intervention approvals fell further than the rate, from 2,099 in 2006 to 765 in 2025. The wells standing on 1 January fell from 16,124 in 2005 to 7,636 in 2025 (article section Interventions by water depth).
- **Light or heavy.** From 2014, at least three in four intervention approvals name a unit that can be classified (82% in 2025). Since then light work has run at 5.4 to 8.5 approvals per 100 standing wells a year and heavy work at 2.5 to 4.8. Light work averaged 7.6 in 2014-2019 and 5.6 in 2023-2025; heavy work averaged 3.8 and 2.8.
- **Depth.** Deepwater wells are fewer and more often subsea, and the operators' own estimates on their permits put a well-work job at a median 14 days at 1,500 m and deeper against 3 under 100 m.
