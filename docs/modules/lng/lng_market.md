## Global Production Capacity & Facilities

### Current Capacity Overview

U.S. LNG exports are projected to increase by 19% to 14.2 billion cubic feet per day in 2025 and by 15% to 16.4 Bcf/d in 2026. The United States became the world's largest LNG exporter in 2023 and maintained this position in 2024.

### Major Production Regions

**United States:**
- Five major LNG export projects under construction with combined capacity of 9.7 Bcf/d: Plaquemines (Phases I and II), Corpus Christi Stage III, Golden Pass, Rio Grande (Phase I), and Port Arthur (Phase I)
- Corpus Christi Stage 3 produced its first LNG cargo in February 2025, with seven midscale trains totaling 1.3 Bcf/d nominal capacity
- Plaquemines LNG Phase 1 started exports in December 2024, with Phase 2 beginning production in July 2025

**Qatar:**
- Qatar has embarked on a massive expansion program to boost liquefaction capacity from 77 million tonnes per annum to 142 MTPA by 2030
- North Field East Expansion (32 MTPA) construction commenced in October 2023, with first gas expected in 2025 or 2026
- The expansion includes three phases: North Field East (32 MTPA), North Field South (16 MTPA), and North Field West (16 MTPA)
- Qatar has one of the lowest unit costs of LNG production globally, providing a competitive advantage

**Canada:**
- LNG Canada (1.8 Bcf/d capacity) plans to start LNG exports from Train 1 in summer 2025
- Woodfibre LNG (0.3 Bcf/d capacity) targets startup in 2027, and Cedar LNG (0.4 Bcf/d) expects to begin exports in 2028

**Australia:**
- Australia is the second largest global LNG exporter with 111 bcm exported in 2023 and capacity of 120 bcm
- Limited new capacity planned, with Pluto LNG expansion adding 7 bcm capacity expected in 2026
- Declining feedstock supplies led to shutdowns at Darwin LNG in late 2023 and planned closure of one North West Shelf LNG train in 2024

### Floating LNG (FLNG) Technology

As of early 2025, total operational FLNG capacity stands at 14.35 MTPA. Golar LNG predicts that within the next five years, the number of FLNG units may nearly double, with approximately 18 FLNG units globally by 2030, producing over 60 million tons annually.

**Key Advantages:**
- Enables monetization of remote offshore gas fields that would be economically unviable with traditional onshore infrastructure
- Eliminates need for extensive pipelines and onshore facilities, reducing capital costs and environmental impact
- Can be redeployed to newly discovered offshore fields, reducing time to bring fields online

**Recent Developments:**
- Republic of Congo delivered its first LNG cargo in February 2024 from Eni's Tango FLNG unit (0.6 MTPA capacity)
- Eni's Nguya FLNG facility will add 2.4 MTPA, bringing Congo's total capacity to 3 MTPA by end of 2025

## Supply-Demand Dynamics

### Supply Outlook

Between 2025 and 2030, more than 300 bcm/yr of new LNG export capacity is expected to come online from projects already at FID or under construction, representing the largest capacity wave in LNG history.

**Final Investment Decisions (FIDs):**
- Between 2019 and September 2025, nearly 380 bcm/yr of new LNG export capacity reached FID, averaging about 55 bcm/yr annually?more than double the 2014-2018 period
- In 2025, the US dominated FIDs, accounting for about 85% of sanctioned capacity after the lifting of the permitting pause in January

### Demand Outlook

**Asia-Pacific:**
- Global LNG demand is forecast to rise by around 60% by 2040, largely driven by economic growth in Asia
- Asia's gas demand is expected to rise by more than 4% in 2026, accounting for around half of global gas demand growth
- Asia's LNG demand projected to nearly double over 25 years, growing from 270 MTPA in 2024 to 510 MTPA in 2050

**China:**
- China reclaimed its position as the world's largest LNG importer in 2023, though purchases remain 10% below 2021 levels
- China's natural gas demand declined by an estimated 1% year-on-year in the first half of 2025, with LNG imports plummeting by more than 20%

**Europe:**
- European gas demand is forecast to fall by 11% between 2023 and 2030, with LNG imports forecasted to peak in 2025
- Europe's LNG imports rose by 25% year-on-year in the first half of 2025, reaching an all-time high of 92 bcm
- European LNG imports declined 21.22 MT year-on-year to 100.07 MT in 2024, driven by high storage levels and sluggish demand

**Japan & South Korea:**
- In Japan, LNG imports have fallen 20% since 2018, with demand falling 8% in 2023
- South Korea's LNG imports fell almost 5% in 2023, with long-term plans envisioning imports falling 20% through the mid-2030s

### Market Oversupply Risk

The IEA forecasts global LNG liquefaction capacity will increase by almost 50%, from 580 billion cubic metres now to 850 bcm in 2030. The global LNG market is forecast to enter a phase of oversupply from 2027 onward, with total LNG supply projected to exceed demand by 15 million metric tons in 2030.

**Key Concerns:**
- About one-third of LNG capacity under construction remains uncontracted, likely to be sold into spot markets
- During the last major capacity surge from 2017-2020, oversaturated markets triggered sharp price declines and weak profits for many LNG suppliers
- Project delays and sanctions have decreased the extent of anticipated oversupply, but the market remains on track for more supply than demand from 2027 onwards

## Pricing Outlook

European TTF prices are projected to average $13.46/MMBtu in 2025, while Asian LNG front-month prices are projected to average $12.89/MMBtu. Europe is expected to remain the premium market for flexible LNG supplies throughout most of 2025.

**Price Drivers:**
- Tighter market fundamentals in the first half of 2025 supported higher gas prices, with European hub and Asian LNG spot prices averaging 40% and 28% above levels in the first half of 2024
- By mid-2023, prices dropped to the $10-$14/MMBtu range from $30-$40/MMBtu in 2022
- Lower supply and increased competition expected to keep prices elevated through 2025

## Key Challenges & Risks

1. **Cost Inflation:** EPC-cost inflation and labor shortages have caused 20-30% cost jumps, with just 14.8 MTPA of capacity reaching FID in 2024

2. **Project Delays:** If under-construction projects adhered to original FID timelines, supply would be 20-36 million tons higher than base case over 2025-2027

3. **Geopolitical Risks:** Russian sanctions have blocked Arctic LNG 2 deliveries and significantly slowed construction progress

4. **Renewable Competition:** Japan's LNG imports have declined 20% since 2018, with hydrogen economics potentially reducing South Korea's purchases similarly by the mid-2030s

5. **Infrastructure Delays:** Extensive delays for new LNG import infrastructure projects in emerging Asia have slowed structural demand growth

## Market Outlook Summary

The global LNG market stands at a pivotal juncture. While massive capacity expansions from the United States and Qatar will dramatically increase supply through 2028, demand growth may not keep pace, particularly as developed Asian markets reduce consumption and Europe's gas demand declines. The anticipated oversupply from 2027 onwards could lead to price weakness and financial challenges for producers, echoing the 2017-2020 period. 

However, emerging Asian markets?especially India, Vietnam, and the Philippines?represent significant growth potential if LNG remains affordable. The success of floating LNG technology in monetizing stranded offshore reserves offers new supply flexibility, while technological innovations and carbon capture initiatives position LNG as a transition fuel in the path to net-zero emissions.

Market participants should closely monitor FID activity, long-term offtake agreements, spot price levels, and the execution timeline of major projects to navigate this dynamic and increasingly competitive landscape.
