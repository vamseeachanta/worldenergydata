Published workbooks, reports, prices, canonical financial calculations and the activity-code day basis are unchanged. Matching the golden header establishes schema agreement, not agreement with its values.

Worked MIRR example

Inputs are synthetic monthly cashflows [-100, 50, -110, 200] in USD, a finance rate of 10.0% per month and a reinvestment rate of 20.0% per month. The supplied annual effective rates are (1.112 − 1) and (1.212 − 1). These are demonstration inputs, not economic assumptions.

The Microsoft MIRR definition compounds positive flows to the terminal period at the reinvestment rate and discounts negative flows to inception at the finance rate. With three intervals, FV+ = 50 × 1.22 + 200 = 272 USD and |PV−| = 100 + 110 / 1.12 = 2100/11 USD. MIRR = (FV+ / |PV−|)1/3 − 1 = (748/525)1/3 − 1, or 12.5% per month. Annual MIRR = (748/525)4 − 1, or 312.1%.

Source distinction: Principles of Finance, OpenStax, 2022 web edition, section 16.3, establishes the discounted-cashflow/IRR basis. The separate-rate MIRR definition is taken from Microsoft's web reference, not attributed to that textbook. A directly accessible textbook MIRR definition has not been established; independent textbook qualification remains NOT_EVALUATED. The test's expected result is the rational hand calculation above, not another call to the implementation.

Worked archive example

A synthetic archive has first.txt with 10 bbl for API 101 and second.csv with the same record plus 20 bbl for API 102. Reading only the first member yields 10 bbl. Reading all members and removing exact repeated records yields two records and 10 + 20 = 30 bbl. The 20 bbl loss follows directly from the supplied fixture. Distinct completion records remain additive; no API/month-only deduplication is used.
